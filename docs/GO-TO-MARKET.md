## 1. Why now (the regulatory tailwind)

This product launches into the strongest demand window document
accessibility has ever had:

- **ADA Title II web rule (US)**: DOJ adopted WCAG 2.1 AA for ALL state and
  local government digital content — explicitly including "conventional
  electronic documents" (PDF, Word, PowerPoint). On April 20, 2026 the DOJ
  extended the deadlines by one year: **April 26, 2027** for entities serving
  50,000+ people; **April 26, 2028** for smaller entities and special
  districts. Translation: every city, county, school district, public
  university, transit agency, and state portal in America has a hard,
  dated, federal mandate ~10 months out — and they are budgeting for it
  RIGHT NOW. The extension is your selling window, not a reprieve.
- **HHS Section 504 rule**: health and human services programs face their own
  accessibility deadlines (May 2026 wave already in force) — hospitals,
  clinics, and insurers handling federal funds.
- **Section 508**: federal agencies and every contractor selling to them have
  a standing obligation — document accessibility is a procurement
  checkbox that blocks deals.
- **European Accessibility Act**: in force since June 2025, enforcement
  intensifying through 2026 with fines up to €3M — relevant for any US firm
  selling into the EU.
- **Market size**: digital accessibility software is ~$900M (2025) growing
  to a projected ~$1.4B by 2027.

## 2. The competitive wedge (why 508 Agent wins deals)

| Alternative | What it costs | Weakness 508 Agent exploits |
|---|---|---|
| Manual remediation services (Accessible.org, AbleDocs, 247AD…) | **$5–25/page** ($7–11.50 typical) | Slow (days/weeks turnaround), unaffordable at archive scale |
| Tool licenses (Equidox, CommonLook) | **$2,500–10,000/seat/year + per-page fees** | Expensive before page 1; steep learning curve; still labor-heavy |
| Overlay widgets (accessiBe etc.) | $490–thousands/yr | Don't touch documents at all; legally discredited |
| Adobe Acrobat Pro | $marginal | Manual, per-document, requires a trained human |

**508 Agent's position: instant, self-serve, honest, and 10–100x cheaper.**
A 20-page PDF that costs $150–500 to remediate manually costs **5 credits
(≈ a few dollars)** here, finishes in seconds, and ships with a verifiable
certificate. The honesty architecture (scores only count fixes that persist;
public certificate verification) is a *differentiator against overlay-vendor
distrust* — lead with it.

Pricing guidance: you are deliberately underpriced for land-grab. Hold
$99/$499 through the first ~20 customers, then test Team $149 / Business
$749 — still 90% below an Equidox seat.

## 3. Who to sell to (ranked ICPs)

1. **City/county clerks & ADA coordinators, 50k+ population** (deadline
   April 2027). Pain: thousands of board minutes, agendas, forms. Search
   "[state] city ADA coordinator" — these people are findable, mandated,
   and budgeted. Entry: free audit of 3 documents from their own website.
2. **Public school districts & community colleges / universities** (Title II
   covers them; boards are panicking). Entry via disability-services
   offices and webmasters.
3. **Government contractors** (Section 508 procurement blocker). They lose
   bids over inaccessible proposals/deliverables. Sell Business plan as
   "bid insurance."
4. **Healthcare orgs under HHS rules** (patient forms, statements).
5. **Agencies/consultancies that produce documents for the above** — your
   channel play: white-label-ish reseller arrangement (they buy Business,
   bill their clients per page at manual-service rates, pocket the spread).

## 4. The Sunriver Consulting wrapper (sell the service, not the login)

Don't sell "software" to governments — sell a **productized compliance
service** powered by your software. Sunriver Consulting offers:

- **"Document Accessibility Assessment" — $1,500 flat.** You run their 25
  highest-traffic documents through 508 Agent, deliver the branded findings
  report + certificates for the clean ones + a remediation quote. Takes you
  an afternoon; the tool does the work. This is the door-opener SKU.
- **"Remediation Sprint" — $0.99–2.50/page** (vs the $5–25 market): bulk
  remediation through the tool with your human pass over the
  pending-manual items (contrast judgments, OCR proofread). Still 5–10x
  margin on your time because the tool does 80%.
- **"Compliance Retainer" — $750–2,000/mo**: monthly re-audits of their
  document feed, certificates on file for their auditor, priority
  turnaround. This is recurring revenue stacked on the SaaS.
- Self-serve SaaS remains the low-touch tier for orgs that want DIY — and
  the free analyzer is your lead magnet for everything above.

Why this works: government buyers can sole-source small consulting
engagements far more easily than they can procure software; $1,500
assessments fly under procurement thresholds; and Sunriver becomes the
trusted human in the loop, which is what compliance buyers actually pay for.

**A. Outbound (highest ROI first 90 days, $0 spend)**
- Build a list: every city/county 50k+ in your state(s), the ADA

- The email: *"The DOJ's web rule now covers your PDFs — deadline April 26,
  2027. I ran 3 documents from [their-site].gov through our analyzer; two
  have blockers a screen reader can't get past. 15 minutes and I'll show
  you exactly what they are and what fixing your backlog would cost —

  That personalized free audit IS the demo and costs you nothing.

  league / clerk associations (their newsletters and conferences are cheap

**B. LinkedIn (Sunriver brand-building)**

**C. Search (intent capture)**

  cheaper than general SaaS; start $20–30/day, send traffic to a landing

- SEO content (one article/week, written from your real engine knowledge):

**D. Marketplaces/partnerships (months 2–3)**
- List Sunriver in state procurement vendor pools and on SAM.gov.

## 6. The demo that closes (10 minutes)

1. Open THEIR website, download one of THEIR PDFs live.
2. Drag it into 508 Agent → findings appear with plain-English explanations
   and WCAG/508 citations. ("This is what a screen reader user hits.")
3. Approve fixes → download → click **"Verify the fix — free re-audit"** →
   issues drop on screen. That before/after moment is the close.
4. Issue the certificate → open the public verification link. ("This is
   what you hand your auditor.")
5. Quote: assessment now, sprint for the backlog, retainer for the future.

## 7. Objections & answers

- *"We have an overlay widget."* Overlays don't touch documents; the DOJ
  rule explicitly covers conventional electronic documents. Show their PDF
  failing WITH the overlay active.
- *"Our documents are old — aren't archives exempt?"* Pre-existing archived
  documents have a limited exception **unless they're used to apply for or
  participate in current services** — application forms, current policies,
  meeting agendas are all in scope. The assessment tells them which is which.
- *"We'll just use Acrobat."* Acrobat is manual, per-document, and needs a
  trained operator; price their backlog at staff hourly rates vs the sprint.
- *"How do we know fixes are real?"* The score only counts fixes verified in
  the output bytes; every certificate has a public verification URL; the
  re-audit button proves it on their own file. No competitor leads with
  honesty mechanics — this lands hard with compliance officers.
- *"Is automated remediation a 'formal conformance determination'?"* No —
  and the product says so explicitly (that honesty is why they can trust
  the rest). Position the Sunriver human pass as the judgment layer.

## 8. 30 / 60 / 90 (solo-operator pacing)

**Days 1–30 — prove the loop**
- Deploy production (HANDOFF.md §5), run the Stripe test, enable OCR.
- Point support@/privacy@/security@ at real inboxes.
- Build the 100-org outbound list; send 10 personalized free-audit emails
  per week; book 5 demos; close 2 paid assessments ($3k).
- Start LinkedIn cadence + publish 2 SEO articles.

**Days 31–60 — first recurring revenue**
- Convert assessments → 1 remediation sprint + 1 retainer.
- Turn the best before/after into a one-page case study (anonymized if
  needed) — attach to every email.
- Start the $20/day Google Ads test on the analyzer landing page.
- Apply to your state's vendor pool; register on SAM.gov.

**Days 61–90 — scale what worked**
- Double down on the channel with the best demo-to-close rate.
- Raise SaaS prices for new signups if close rate >40% (signal you're
  underpriced).
- Pitch 3 municipal-web agencies on the reseller arrangement.
- Target: $5–10k MRR equivalent across SaaS + retainers, pipeline of 20
  assessments.

**Metrics that matter**: free audits sent → demos booked → assessments paid
(the funnel); then assessment → sprint/retainer conversion; SaaS signups
from the free analyzer are gravy until the consulting flywheel spins.
